# History and Evolution of Artificial Intelligence

> A research timeline of pivotal ideas, technologies, systems, and breakthroughs in artificial intelligence, from early computational models to modern generative AI and large language models.

## Overview

This timeline contains **31 milestones**, spanning **1943–2024**. It covers AI foundations, symbolic AI, machine learning, neural networks, deep learning, computer vision, natural language processing, reinforcement learning, generative AI, large language models, and AI for science.

## Historical timeline

| Year | Milestone | Subfield | Significance |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1943 | [A Logical Calculus of the Ideas Immanent in Nervous Activity](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02478259) | Artificial neural networks | Warren McCulloch and Walter Pitts published a mathematical model of simplified neurons using threshold logic. It established an influential foundation for neural-network research. *(Source/date: McCulloch & Pitts, Bulletin of Mathematical Biophysics (1943))* |
| 1950 | [Alan Turing proposes the imitation game](https://doi.org/10.1093/mind/LIX.236.433) | AI foundations; machine intelligence | In “Computing Machinery and Intelligence,” Turing reframed the question “Can machines think?” as an operational test based on a machine’s ability to imitate human conversation. *(Source/date: Turing, Mind (1950))* |
| 1956 | [Dartmouth Summer Research Project on Artificial Intelligence](https://www-formal.stanford.edu/jmc/history/dartmouth/dartmouth.html) | Artificial intelligence (field formation) | The Dartmouth proposal and summer workshop, organized by John McCarthy, Marvin Minsky, Nathaniel Rochester and Claude Shannon, helped establish AI as a named research field. The proposal dates to 1955; the workshop took place in summer 1956. *(Source/date: Dartmouth proposal (1955); historical accounts of the 1956 workshop)* |
| 1957 | [Rosenblatt’s perceptron](https://doi.org/10.1037/h0042519) | Machine learning; neural networks | Frank Rosenblatt introduced the perceptron, an influential trainable model for pattern classification, helping launch early research into learning machines. *(Source/date: Rosenblatt, Psychological Review (1958); early Mark I work began in 1957)* |
| 1958 | [LISP programming language created](https://www-formal.stanford.edu/jmc/history/lisp/lisp.html) | Symbolic AI; programming languages | John McCarthy designed LISP, which became a major language for symbolic reasoning, knowledge representation and AI research. *(Source/date: McCarthy, “History of LISP”)* |
| 1966 | [ELIZA chatbot published](https://doi.org/10.1145/365153.365168) | Natural language processing; conversational systems | Joseph Weizenbaum’s ELIZA used pattern matching and scripted responses to simulate conversation, demonstrating both the appeal and limitations of language-based interaction. *(Source/date: Weizenbaum, Communications of the ACM (1966))* |
| 1969 | [Perceptrons highlights limits of single-layer models](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262631112/perceptrons/) | Neural networks; machine learning | Marvin Minsky and Seymour Papert’s book analyzed limitations of perceptrons, including their inability to represent XOR with a single layer. It contributed to reduced enthusiasm and funding for some neural-network work, though it did not alone cause an AI winter. *(Source/date: Minsky & Papert, Perceptrons (1969))* |
| 1972 | [PROLOG developed](https://www.doc.ic.ac.uk/~rak/) | Logic programming; knowledge representation | Alain Colmerauer’s group developed PROLOG in the early 1970s, with Robert Kowalski contributing to its logical foundations. It became important in symbolic AI and expert systems. *(Source/date: Historical accounts of PROLOG; Kowalski’s work on logic for problem solving)* |
| 1973 | [Lighthill report prompts UK AI funding retrenchment](https://www.chilton-computing.org.uk/inf/literature/reports/lighthill_report/) | AI policy; research evaluation | The UK Science Research Council report criticized progress in several AI areas and contributed to cuts in UK AI funding. It is commonly associated with the first AI winter, although the slowdown had multiple causes. *(Source/date: James Lighthill, “Artificial Intelligence: A General Survey” (1973))* |
| 1980 | [XCON expert system deployed at Digital Equipment Corporation](https://doi.org/10.1016/0004-3702(82)90021-2) | Expert systems; knowledge-based AI | XCON (originally R1), developed by John McDermott and colleagues, configured VAX computer systems and demonstrated commercial value from rule-based expert systems. *(Source/date: McDermott, “R1: A Rule-Based Configurer of Computer Systems” (1982))* |
| 1986 | [Backpropagation popularized for multilayer neural networks](https://doi.org/10.1038/323533a0) | Deep learning; neural networks | David Rumelhart, Geoffrey Hinton and Ronald Williams published a widely influential treatment showing how backpropagation could train multilayer networks by propagating error gradients backward. *(Source/date: Nature (1986))* |
| 1989 | [Convolutional neural networks applied to handwritten digit recognition](https://doi.org/10.1162/neco.1989.1.4.541) | Computer vision; deep learning | Yann LeCun and collaborators demonstrated gradient-based learning with convolutional networks for document and digit recognition, helping establish CNNs as a powerful vision architecture. *(Source/date: LeCun et al., Neural Computation (1989))* |
| 1997 | [IBM Deep Blue defeats world chess champion Garry Kasparov](https://www.ibm.com/history/deep-blue) | Search; game-playing AI | Deep Blue defeated Kasparov in a six-game match, showing the strength of specialized search, evaluation functions and high-performance computing in a bounded domain. It was not a general-intelligence system. *(Source/date: IBM historical account; match concluded May 1997)* |
| 2006 | [Deep belief nets renew interest in deep learning](https://doi.org/10.1162/neco.2006.18.7.1527) | Deep learning; neural networks | Geoffrey Hinton and collaborators showed how layer-wise unsupervised pretraining could help train deeper networks, contributing to the modern deep-learning revival. *(Source/date: Hinton, Osindero & Teh, Neural Computation (2006))* |
| 2009 | [ImageNet dataset introduced](https://doi.org/10.1109/CVPR.2009.5206848) | Computer vision; data-centric machine learning | Fei-Fei Li’s team introduced a large, hierarchically organized image dataset that enabled large-scale benchmarking and helped make data and compute central to progress in visual recognition. *(Source/date: Deng et al., CVPR (2009))* |
| 2012 | [AlexNet wins the ImageNet Large Scale Visual Recognition Challenge](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/4824-imagenet-classification-with-deep-convolutional-neural-networks) | Deep learning; computer vision | Alex Krizhevsky, Ilya Sutskever and Geoffrey Hinton’s deep CNN achieved a major improvement in image classification, using GPUs, ReLU activations, data augmentation and dropout. It catalyzed broad adoption of deep learning in vision. *(Source/date: Krizhevsky, Sutskever & Hinton, NeurIPS (2012))* |
| 2014 | [Generative adversarial networks (GANs) introduced](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/5423-generative-adversarial-nets) | Generative AI; deep learning | Ian Goodfellow and co-authors proposed two neural networks—a generator and discriminator—trained adversarially. GANs became a major approach to synthesizing realistic images and other data. *(Source/date: Goodfellow et al., NeurIPS (2014))* |
| 2014 | [Sequence-to-sequence learning and neural attention advance](https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.0473) | NLP; neural machine translation | Encoder–decoder models and attention mechanisms improved neural translation and sequence tasks. Bahdanau, Cho and Bengio’s 2014 work made attention a key step toward later Transformer architectures. *(Source/date: Bahdanau, Cho & Bengio (2014; published 2015))* |
| 2016 | [AlphaGo defeats Lee Sedol](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature16961) | Reinforcement learning; search; game AI | DeepMind’s AlphaGo combined deep neural networks, Monte Carlo tree search and reinforcement learning. Its 4–1 match victory demonstrated the power of combining learned intuition with search in Go. *(Source/date: Silver et al., Nature (2016); match March 2016)* |
| 2017 | [“Attention Is All You Need” introduces the Transformer](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03762) | NLP; foundation-model architecture | Vaswani and co-authors proposed the Transformer, based on self-attention rather than recurrence or convolution. Its parallelizable design became the dominant architecture for many large language and multimodal models. *(Source/date: Vaswani et al., NeurIPS (2017))* |
| 2018 | [BERT brings bidirectional pretraining to NLP](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.04805) | NLP; transfer learning; language models | Google researchers introduced BERT, pretrained using masked-language modeling and then fine-tuned for downstream tasks. It helped make large-scale pretraining and task-specific fine-tuning standard in NLP. *(Source/date: Devlin et al., NAACL-HLT (2019); arXiv preprint 2018)* |
| 2020 | [GPT-3 demonstrates few-shot language capabilities at scale](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.14165) | Large language models; generative AI | OpenAI described a 175-billion-parameter autoregressive language model that performed many tasks from text prompts and examples, intensifying interest in scaling and in-context learning. *(Source/date: Brown et al., NeurIPS (2020); preprint May 2020)* |
| 2020 | [AlphaFold 2 achieves a major breakthrough in protein-structure prediction](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-021-03819-2) | AI for science; computational biology | DeepMind’s system achieved highly accurate protein-structure predictions in CASP14, illustrating how machine learning could transform scientific research beyond conventional software tasks. *(Source/date: Jumper et al., Nature (2021); CASP14 results announced 2020)* |
| 2020 | [Denoising diffusion probabilistic models gain prominence](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.11239) | Generative AI; image generation | Ho, Jain and Abbeel presented a practical diffusion-model framework that helped establish diffusion as a leading family of generative models for high-quality image synthesis. *(Source/date: Ho, Jain & Abbeel, NeurIPS (2020))* |
| 2021 | [DALL·E demonstrates text-to-image generation](https://openai.com/index/dall-e/) | Generative AI; multimodal models | OpenAI introduced a 12-billion-parameter GPT-3-based model that generated images from text descriptions, bringing text-conditioned image synthesis to wider attention. *(Source/date: OpenAI research announcement (January 2021))* |
| 2022 | [Stable Diffusion released publicly](https://github.com/CompVis/stable-diffusion) | Generative AI; latent diffusion | The public release of Stable Diffusion made powerful text-to-image generation more accessible to developers and users, accelerating experimentation and adoption of open generative image models. *(Source/date: CompVis / Stability AI model release and documentation (2022))* |
| 2022 | [ChatGPT launched](https://openai.com/index/chatgpt/) | Large language models; conversational AI | OpenAI released ChatGPT as a dialogue-oriented system based on instruction-following models and reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF), rapidly popularizing public interaction with LLMs. *(Source/date: OpenAI launch announcement (30 November 2022))* |
| 2023 | [GPT-4 announced](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.08774) | Large language models; multimodal AI | OpenAI described GPT-4 as a large multimodal model that accepts image and text inputs and produces text, with improved performance on a range of professional and academic benchmarks. *(Source/date: OpenAI, GPT-4 Technical Report and announcement (2023))* |
| 2023 | [Llama 2 released for research and commercial use under a community license](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.09288) | Open-weight LLMs; generative AI | Meta’s Llama 2 release broadened access to capable openly available model weights and helped accelerate an ecosystem of fine-tuning, evaluation and deployment outside a small number of model providers. *(Source/date: Touvron et al., 2023)* |
| 2024 | [AlphaFold 3 extends prediction to biomolecular interactions](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07487-w) | AI for science; computational biology | DeepMind and Isomorphic Labs presented a model predicting structures and interactions involving proteins, DNA, RNA, small molecules and other biomolecular components, broadening AI’s role in molecular research. *(Source/date: Abramson et al., Nature (2024))* |
| 2024 | [AlphaProof and AlphaGeometry 2 demonstrate advances in mathematical reasoning](https://deepmind.google/discover/blog/ai-solves-imo-problems-at-silver-medal-level/) | AI reasoning; symbolic and neural methods | Google DeepMind reported systems that solved several problems from the 2024 International Mathematical Olympiad at silver-medal standard when considered together, highlighting progress and remaining limits in formal and mathematical reasoning. *(Source/date: Google DeepMind research announcement (July 2024))* |

## Key concepts

- **Artificial intelligence (AI):** The broad field concerned with building machines that perform tasks associated with intelligent behavior, including reasoning, perception, language use, planning, and decision-making.
- **Machine learning (ML):** A subfield of AI in which systems learn patterns from data or experience rather than relying only on explicitly hand-written rules.
- **Deep learning:** A branch of machine learning based on neural networks with multiple layers that learn complex representations from data.
- **Generative AI:** AI systems that create new content—such as text, images, audio, video, or code—based on patterns learned during training.
- **Large language models (LLMs):** Large neural models trained on language data to process and generate text. Many modern LLMs use Transformer architectures and can perform tasks from prompts, examples, or instruction tuning.
- **Reinforcement learning (RL):** A machine-learning approach in which an agent learns to make decisions through interaction with an environment and feedback such as rewards.

These areas overlap rather than replace one another. For example, modern generative AI often uses deep learning; many LLMs are Transformer-based; and reinforcement learning can be used to refine model behavior.

## Research notes

### Scope

A selective global timeline of foundational AI ideas, influential systems, major machine-learning methods, generative AI, LLMs and AI for science. It is not an exhaustive list of every contribution.

### Date convention

Year refers to the event, first public introduction, preprint, or competition result as indicated in the source/date note. A paper's publication year can differ from the first announcement or event year.

### Terminology

AI is the broad field. Machine learning is a subfield in which systems learn patterns from data or interaction. Deep learning uses multilayer neural networks. Generative AI creates new content. Large language models (LLMs) are large neural language models, commonly Transformer-based, trained on text and sometimes other modalities.

### Historical caution

The timeline avoids treating any single event as the sole cause of an AI winter or as the invention of an entire field. Dates sometimes refer to an early demonstration, with a later peer-reviewed publication noted separately.

### Research limitation

This document was compiled from established primary papers, official research announcements and historical sources. Live web browsing was not available in this session, so links are supplied for independent verification rather than represented as freshly checked during this session.

## Notes on historical dates

Some milestones have more than one relevant date: an idea may have been demonstrated or announced before its peer-reviewed paper appeared. Where this matters, the timeline records the event or first-publication year and explains the distinction in the source/date note. For example, the Dartmouth proposal was written in 1955 and the workshop took place in 1956; AlphaFold 2's CASP14 result was announced in 2020 and its major journal paper appeared in 2021.
